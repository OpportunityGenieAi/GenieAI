How to write a winning scholarship motivation letter

A motivation letter explains why you deserve the scholarship and what you will do with it. Selection panels read many, so clarity and specifics win.

Use a simple structure

  • Opening: who you are, what you apply for, and one sentence on why.
  • Background: the studies, work or projects that prepared you.
  • Fit: why this programme and this country, using details from its website.
  • Goals: what you will do after the scholarship.
  • Closing: thank the panel and restate your commitment.

Be specific

  • Replace "I am passionate about health" with what you did, when, and what changed.
  • Use numbers where you can: students taught, patients reached, funds raised.
  • Mention the exact programme name and one thing that attracted you to it.

Common mistakes

  • Sending the same letter to every programme.
  • Repeating your CV line by line.
  • Long stories about hardship with no link to your goals.
  • Spelling errors and passing the length limit.
Stay under the word limit if one is given, and keep to one page when none is stated.

Before you submit

  • Read it aloud and cut anything that does not help your case.
  • Ask a lecturer or mentor to read it.
  • Check the name of the scholarship in every paragraph.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a motivation letter be?

Follow the programme limit. If none is given, one page, about 400 to 600 words, is typical.

Is a motivation letter the same as a statement of purpose?

They overlap. Statements of purpose focus more on academic and research plans, motivation letters on you and your fit.
