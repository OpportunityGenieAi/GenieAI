Scholarships without IELTS: how to apply without the test

IELTS is not the only way to show your English. Many universities and scholarship programmes accept alternatives, but the rules differ, so check each one before you apply.

Common alternatives to IELTS

  • A medium of instruction (MOI) certificate from a university where your degree was taught in English.
  • TOEFL, Duolingo English Test, PTE Academic or Cambridge exams, where the university accepts them.
  • Completing an earlier degree in an English-speaking country.
  • A pre-sessional English course offered by the university.
  • An interview or internal language assessment at some institutions.
Acceptance is decided by the university or the funder, not by us. An MOI certificate is accepted at some universities and refused at others.

How to check the rule for a programme

  • Open the "admission requirements" or "English language" page on the official site.
  • Search the page for "MOI", "medium of instruction" or "waiver".
  • Write to the admissions office and ask in one clear sentence, keeping the reply.
  • Ask whether the waiver also applies to the scholarship, since some funders have their own language rule.

Study in countries that are often flexible

  • Some universities in Germany, Italy, Hungary, Turkey, Taiwan and South Korea teach in English and use their own language checks.
  • Many African and Asian students use an MOI letter for admission and then take a test later for the visa.
This changes between universities and years. Treat it as a place to start looking, not as a promise.

Warning signs

  • Any site that sells an IELTS certificate or "guaranteed waiver" is a scam.
  • A real waiver comes from the university, in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a scholarship without IELTS?

Yes, if the university and funder accept another proof of English such as an MOI certificate or a different test. It depends on the programme.

What is an MOI certificate?

A letter from your previous university stating that your studies were taught and examined in English.

Will my visa still need a test?

Sometimes. Visa rules are separate from admission, so check the embassy requirements for your destination.
